The case for a slower, kinder web

8 min read

Every browser asks you to adapt to it. Its buttons, its menus, its idea of where things go. What if it worked the other way around, and the browser adapted to you?

Say what you want in plain words. Bigger text for tired eyes, tabs where you expect them, the colors you like. And it simply happens.

Press ⌘K for the command bar
✎ config.yaml your settings

      
Every change your AI makes is a readable line in one file. Undo is deleting it.

    

  






  

    
Agents built in

    
An AI that can use your browser, not just talk about it.

    
Chats can open tabs, read pages, click and fill things in, with the access you choose: read only, ask first, or full. Ask questions about any page right in find. Schedule a morning brief. Have it watch a page and tap you when something changes.

    
Works with Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and more. No subscription? Valerie sets up free AI for you.

Install
Get Valerie
Free and open source. Pick whichever way you like. After that, Valerie keeps itself up to date.

  

    
SmoothestOne line in Terminal
Downloads the latest Valerie, checks it's genuine, puts it in Applications and opens it.

      
curl -fsSL https://cullen-b.github.io/valerie/install.sh | bash

    
Download
Open the disk image and drag Valerie into Applications. Linux and Windows builds are on the releases page.

       Valerie.dmg
      All downloads

    
Homebrew
No need to brew upgrade: Valerie updates itself.

      
brew install --cask cullen-b/valerie/valerie

  

  
Valerie isn't notarized by Apple yet, so the first time you open a downloaded or Homebrew copy, macOS may say it can't check it. Click Done, then System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway. That's once; the Terminal line doesn't need it.
