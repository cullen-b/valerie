Local man has 400 tabs open, will read them “later”

He has said this every day since 2019.

Advertisement · 7 browser tricks tech companies hate

“Some of them are recipes,” he said, scrolling past a tab from March. “One is a fridge I might buy.” Experts say most people keep tabs open as a to-do list they never look at.

The tabs date back to a single afternoon in 2019, when he went looking for a good pair of running shoes. He found eleven candidates, four reviews of those candidates, a forum thread arguing about the reviews, and a video essay on the history of the running shoe. He has not gone running.

Friends describe the collection as “a museum.” There is a tab for a concert that has already happened, a tab for a flight that has already landed, and a tab for an article titled “Why You Should Close Your Tabs,” which he says he is saving for the right moment.

His laptop fan, reached for comment, was too loud to understand.

Researchers who study the habit say it is less about reading and more about hope. Each open tab is a small promise: that there will be a quiet Sunday, that the recipe will get cooked, that the fridge will be bought. Closing it means admitting the Sunday may never come.

“I tried bookmarks once,” he said. “But then I'd have to remember I have bookmarks.” He has since started a second window, for tabs about organizing his tabs.

His sister, who uses a phone and nothing else, says she has never seen the bottom of his tab bar. “It's tabs all the way down,” she said. “Some of them don't even have icons anymore. They're just little grey squares.”

Asked which tab he would save in a fire, he chose a page about how to put out fires, which he opened in 2021 and has not read.

Psychologists call this “tab debt.” Like any debt, it grows quietly. One tab becomes three, three becomes a window, and one day the browser asks whether you'd like to restore 412 tabs from your last session, and you click yes, because what else would you click.

There are ways out. Some people close everything on Fridays. Some let the browser tidy away tabs they haven't touched in a week. Some declare bankruptcy and start again. He has considered all three, in separate tabs.

For now, the fridge is still on sale. The running shoes have been discontinued. The concert has been over for three years. And somewhere near the end of the tab bar, past the little grey squares, a recipe for banana bread is waiting.

“Someday,” he said, and opened a new tab.

At press time, he had opened this article in a new tab to read later.
